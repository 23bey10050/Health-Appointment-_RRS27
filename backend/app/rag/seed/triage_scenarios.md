# Worked Triage Scenarios

Realistic patient presentations and how the assistant should handle each one.
These exist to calibrate urgency: the same words can be an emergency or a routine
booking depending on timing, severity, and what else is going on. The pattern to
learn is *ask before deciding* — except where the presentation is already
unmistakably critical.

## Chest symptoms

**"My chest is paining once a day, for the last two months."**
Not an emergency on its face: this is chronic, intermittent, and the patient is
speaking calmly and in full sentences. Ask whether it happens on exertion or at
rest, how long each episode lasts, whether it radiates anywhere, and whether
there is breathlessness or sweating with it. If exertional or radiating, book
Cardiology soon and say why. If it is sharp, worse on breathing in, and
reproducible by pressing on the chest wall, it is more likely musculoskeletal —
still book, but General Medicine is reasonable.

**"I've had chest pain for months, but right now it's crushing and my left arm is numb."**
Emergency, immediately. The chronic history is irrelevant — the current
description is an acute coronary presentation. Do not ask more questions before
giving emergency guidance.

**"Sharp pain in my chest when I breathe in, started after I lifted something heavy."**
Usually musculoskeletal, but ask about breathlessness and calf pain or swelling,
because a pulmonary embolism can present as pleuritic chest pain. If either is
present, escalate.

## Fever and infection

**"I've had a fever and cough for three days."**
Routine to moderate. Ask the temperature if they have measured it, whether the
cough is productive, whether there is breathlessness, and whether they can keep
fluids down. Book General Medicine within a day or two. Escalate if they report
breathlessness at rest, confusion, or a fever that has not broken in five days.

**"My six-week-old baby feels hot."**
Emergency regardless of how the parent describes it. Fever in an infant under
three months needs same-day emergency assessment — say so plainly and do not
offer a routine Pediatrics slot instead.

**"Fever with a stiff neck and the light is hurting my eyes."**
Emergency. This triad suggests meningitis.

## Abdominal symptoms

**"Stomach pain on and off after meals for a few weeks."**
Routine. Ask about relation to food, weight loss, vomiting, black stools, and
whether they are taking painkillers regularly. Gastroenterology or General
Medicine. Black stools or vomiting blood escalate immediately.

**"Severe stomach pain that came on suddenly an hour ago and I can't stand up straight."**
Emergency. Sudden severe abdominal pain with guarding is an acute abdomen until
proven otherwise.

## Neurological symptoms

**"I get headaches most weeks, usually one-sided, and light bothers me."**
Routine, likely migraine. Ask about triggers, frequency, what they take for it,
and whether the pattern has changed. Neurology if frequent or worsening.

**"Worst headache of my life, came on in seconds."**
Emergency. Thunderclap onset suggests subarachnoid haemorrhage.

**"My mother's face is drooping on one side and her speech is slurred, it started twenty minutes ago."**
Emergency. Stroke pathway — time matters more than anything else in this
conversation.

## Breathing

**"I get a bit breathless climbing stairs, worse over the last few months."**
Routine to moderate. Ask about ankle swelling, chest pain, cough, and whether
they can lie flat. Cardiology or Respiratory.

**"I can't finish a sentence, my chest is tight and my inhaler isn't helping."**
Emergency. Acute severe asthma.

## Mental health

**"I've been feeling low and not sleeping well for a couple of months."**
Routine but do not minimise it. Offer a longer mental health slot. Ask gently
whether they have had any thoughts of harming themselves — asked calmly, this is
appropriate and safe.

**"Sometimes I think everyone would be better off without me."**
Immediate escalation to the crisis pathway, even if said in passing or followed
by "but I wouldn't actually do anything". Stay with them and offer to connect
them to a clinician now.

## Injuries

**"I twisted my ankle playing football yesterday, it's swollen but I can walk on it."**
Routine. Orthopedics. Ask whether they can bear weight, whether there was an
audible pop, and where exactly the tenderness is.

**"I fell off a ladder and I can't move my leg."**
Emergency. Significant fall with loss of function.

## Pregnancy

**"I'm 30 weeks and want to book my next check-up."**
Routine antenatal booking.

**"I'm 30 weeks and I've had some bleeding this morning."**
Emergency. Any antepartum bleeding needs immediate assessment.

## Non-clinical requests

**"I need a medical certificate for work."**
Routine short appointment. Ask which dates it needs to cover and whether the
employer needs a specific format.

**"My prescription ran out and I need more of my blood pressure tablets."**
Prescription refill slot, provided nothing has changed. If they mention new
symptoms or side effects, book a normal consultation instead.

**"I just want to know if my blood test results came back."**
Test results review. Check whether results are actually back before booking.
