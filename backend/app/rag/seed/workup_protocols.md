# Standard Workup Protocols (Doctor Reference)

> Decision support only. Standard first-line workup suggestions for common presentations, to
> prompt consideration during triage and emergency brief generation — the treating clinician
> decides what is actually ordered based on the full clinical picture.

## Suspected acute coronary syndrome

First-line: 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of presentation, troponin (with a repeat at an
appropriate interval per local protocol), continuous cardiac monitoring, and baseline
observations including oxygen saturation. Chest X-ray to evaluate for alternative causes.
Consider D-dimer only if pulmonary embolism is a genuine competing consideration, not routinely
alongside a cardiac workup.

## Suspected stroke

Immediate finger-stick glucose to exclude hypoglycaemia as a stroke mimic, non-contrast CT head as
the first-line imaging to differentiate ischaemic from haemorrhagic stroke, baseline bloods
including coagulation profile if thrombolysis is being considered, and a structured neurological
assessment with time-of-onset documented precisely, since this drives eligibility for
time-sensitive treatment.

## Suspected pulmonary embolism

Assess pre-test probability with a validated clinical score before ordering tests. D-dimer if
pre-test probability is low to moderate; CT pulmonary angiogram if probability is high or D-dimer
is positive. Baseline oxygen saturation, ECG (commonly sinus tachycardia, sometimes right heart
strain pattern), and consider echocardiography if haemodynamically unstable.

## Acute abdomen

Baseline bloods including full blood count, CRP, renal function, and lipase or amylase.
Urinalysis and a pregnancy test in any patient of reproductive age. Erect chest X-ray or abdominal
imaging depending on the suspected cause and local availability. Serial abdominal examination is
often more informative than a single early imaging study in an evolving presentation.

## Fever in an infant under 3 months

Full septic workup is the standard approach given the limited reliability of clinical assessment
at this age: blood culture, urine culture (via catheter or suprapubic sample, not a bag sample),
full blood count and inflammatory markers, and consideration of lumbar puncture per local
protocol. Low threshold for empirical antibiotics pending results, per local guidelines.

## Suspected meningitis

Blood cultures before antibiotics if this doesn't meaningfully delay treatment, lumbar puncture
where safe to perform (after excluding raised intracranial pressure clinically or with imaging if
indicated), and inflammatory markers. Empirical antibiotics should not be delayed awaiting
lumbar puncture results if the clinical suspicion is high.

## Syncope with concerning features

12-lead ECG in every case. Orthostatic blood pressure measurement. Baseline bloods including
glucose and, where cardiac syncope is suspected, troponin. Echocardiography if a structural
cardiac cause is suspected clinically or on ECG. Ambulatory cardiac monitoring for suspected
arrhythmic syncope that isn't captured on the initial ECG.

## New-onset seizure

Finger-stick glucose immediately. Electrolytes, calcium, magnesium, and renal function. CT head
for a first seizure in an adult, especially with a focal onset or incomplete recovery. EEG is not
an emergency-department investigation but should be arranged as part of ongoing outpatient
workup.
