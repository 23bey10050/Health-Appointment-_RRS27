# Visit Preparation

## What to bring

Bring a government-issued photo ID for your first visit with any doctor, a list of current
medications (or the medications themselves), and any relevant prior reports — recent blood work,
imaging, or discharge summaries from another hospital. If you're a returning patient, your record
is already on file and you generally don't need to bring ID again.

## Arriving on time

Plan to arrive 10 minutes before your scheduled time for a first visit, since there's a short
registration step. Follow-up visits with a doctor you've already seen don't need extra buffer
time — arriving right at your appointment time is fine.

## Fasting requirements

Fasting is only required if your doctor has specifically asked for fasting blood work as part of
this visit — routine consultations never require fasting. If a doctor orders fasting labs during
your visit, they'll tell you the fasting window (usually 8 to 12 hours) and you'd complete that
test on a separate visit to the lab, not during the consultation itself.

## Children's appointments

A parent or legal guardian must accompany any patient under 18 for the full visit. Bring the
child's vaccination record if this is a first visit with a paediatrician, since it helps the
doctor assess whether the child is up to date.

## What to expect during a video consultation

Join from a quiet, well-lit space with a stable internet connection. Have your current medication
list and any relevant reports ready to show on screen if needed. If the doctor determines a
physical examination is necessary partway through, they'll ask you to book an in-person follow-up
rather than trying to complete the assessment remotely.

## Preparing to describe your symptoms

Whether you're booking by voice, by phone, or in the patient portal, it helps to know: when the
symptom started, whether it's constant or comes and goes, what makes it better or worse, and
whether you've taken anything for it already. You don't need to self-diagnose or guess what's
wrong — just describe what you're experiencing in your own words, and the assistant or doctor
will ask the right follow-up questions.
